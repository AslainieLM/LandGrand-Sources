Abstract 
 
The purpose of the study was to assessed the contributions of Digital Game -Based Learning to the numeracy skills 
among Grade 1 learners. It took place at Andales Integrated School, Initao North District, Division of Misamis Oriental 
during the Second Quarter of the School Year 2024-2025. Using a quasi-experimental design, 34 learners were treated 
using Digital Game-Based learning. The mean, standard deviation, and paired t -test were applied to the statement of 
the problem. With the Rapid Mathemati cs Assessment as a tool, the participants were tested before and after the 
treatment. Results indicated that there was a significant shift in scores from the pretest to the posttest, indicating a 
marked improvement in the numeracy skills of the first grade rs following the implementation of digital game -based 
learning strategies. This implied that adding digital games to classroom settings can promote a more engaging and 
successful instructional process. The study offered suggestions for integrating digital game-based learning in 
classroom settings and emphasized how it improved the performance of learners. As a result, school officials may 
keep looking for support to allocate funds for the school’s technology upgrades.  
 
Keywords:  digital game-based learning, pretest, posttest, quasi-experimental design, rapid mathematics assessment 
 
Introduction 
 
At present, first-graders encountered many obstacles as they work to improve their numeracy abilities. A variety of factors, including 
individual learning variations, the school environment, instructional approaches, and developmental factors, might contribute to these 
issues. Thus, gaining a strong conceptual grasp of fundamental numeracy ideas like counting, recognizing numbers, and perform ing 
simple addit ion and subtraction arithmetic operations can be difficult. Understanding the significance and connections between 
numbers may be difficult for certain learners. 
Learners started developing the foundational numeracy skills in Grade 1, which serve as the basis for more complex math ideas in later 
grades. These abilities included recognizing numbers, counting, comprehending quantities, and doing simple arithmetic operations like 
addition and subtraction. For daily chores like handling money, recognizing dimen sions, determining the time, and performing simple 
computations, numeracy abilities were crucial. Learners frequently meet real -life circumstances that can be immediately applied to 
these practical skills. First-grade numeracy proficiency establishes a solid basis for future academic achievement. As they move through 
the educational system, it helped learners interact with arithmetic topics with greater confidence, which enhanced their overall academic 
performance. 
Learners in Grade 1 were still working on their capacity to pay attention and concentrate for prolonged amounts of time. They might 
find it difficult to focus during numeracy exercises, which resulted in unfinished work or computation errors. Learners with varying 
learning needs and skills were co mmonly seen in first -grade classrooms. Uneven academic development and comprehension gaps 
might result from teachers' struggles to deliver differentiated education that was tailored to each learner's unique requirem ents. 
Numerous government initiatives and reforms, such as teacher training programs, curriculum adjustments, and interventions to address 
learning gaps, have been implemented in an effort to increase numeracy abilities. Nonetheless, the effects of these programs may differ 
in various locales and educational institutions. The Guidelines on the Early Language, Literacy, and Numeracy Program, also known 
as Department Order Number 12 series 2015, serve as the foundation for one of the Department of Education's primary attempts to 
close the gaps in nu meracy. The Department of Education's Early Language, Literacy, and Numeracy Program aimed to prepare all 
Filipino children for academic success and lifetime learning by making sure they develop the fundamentals of language, litera cy, and 
numeracy early in their school careers, however, still encountered gaps. 
On the other hand, using digital game -based learning to improve first -graders numeracy skills were entertaining and engaging given 
the state of technology. Due to the growing interest of children in p laying video games, the usage of these games has become a hot 
topic.  Sabirli and Coklar (2020), which investigated how educational games affect learners' academic achievement, found that  
incorporating digital instructional games into the curriculum improv ed learners accessed to the material and boosts their performance 
noticeably. A variety of subjects have been taught through digital games. Topics include language, social science, science, a nd nature, 
either social and soft skills (i.e., science, mathematics, or talents for teamwork) (Andersen & Rustad, 2022).  
Hussein et al. (2022) stated that learning through digital games was a learner -centered method, where instructional objectives and 
materials were interwoven in gaming activities and inspired learner s to study and advance their abilities and expertise by giving them 
fun and engaging experience surroundings for learning. Digital game -based learning will be employed to make sure that learners 
improved their numeracy abilities and grasp mathematical idea s. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the contribution of digital 
game-based learning to the numeracy skills of Grade 1 learners. This study was conducted in Andales Integrated School, Initao Nort h

Research Questions 
The study aimed to assess the contributions of digital game -based learning to the numeracy abilities of Grade 1 learners at Andales

Methodology 
Research Design 
The research design used in this study was the one -group pretest and posttest research design. A pretest -posttest design involved 
measuring the dependent variable twice: once before the implementation of the treatment and once following it (Chiang et al.,  2020). 
To employ this research design, the participants were given pretest to measure their prior knowledge. After the pretest, the researcher 
conducted the math lessons using the Digital Game -Based Learning. After the implementation, posttest was given to  the participants 
to measure the effectiveness of the Digital Game-Based Learning.  
Respondents 
The participants of the study were the Grade 1 learners, which comprised one section with 57 learners of Andales Integrated S chool, 
Initao North District, Divis ion of Misamis Oriental, during the second quarter of the School Year 2024 -2025. With the learners' 
academic performance during the first quarter, the participants were chosen based on their academic performance in Mathematic s. 
Those learners whose grades belong to 75 -79 (fairly satisfactory) and 80 -84 (satisfactory) ratings were the participants of the study. 
Overall, there were 34 participants in the study based on their academic performance in Mathematics. They had the pretest usi ng the 
Rapid Mathematics  Assessment, and they received Digital Game -Based Learning as treatment. According to Rivas et al. (2020), 
pretests gave researchers a baseline against which to compare the findings of posttests. After the treatment, they received a  posttest.  
Using a pret est as part of the sampling process provided the researcher with a special chance to improve the learner's sturdiness and 
comprehension of the target population. Pretesting provided valuable insights that immediately influenced the participants' s election 
process, resulting in a more rigorous and reliable research procedure.  
Instrument 
Based on DM -CT-2024, the Rapid Math Assessment, Comprehensive Rapid Literacy Assessment (CRLA) for Grades 1 –3, and 
Philippine Informal Reading Inventory (Phil -IRI) Assessmen t for Grades 4 –6 was administered nationwide by the Department of 
Education (DepEd) through the Curriculum and Teaching Strand.  
One thorough and effective way to assess pupils' mathematical competency was through the use of Rapid Mathematics Assessment 
(RMA). It was a formative evaluation that gave teachers important information about how well their learners grasp mathematical ideas 
and abilities, empowering them to plan lessons and deliver interventions accordingly. Based on fundamental learning competenc ies, 
the rapid mathematics assessment was intended to pinpoint learners' areas of strength and growth in their mathematical aptitu de. To 
gauge progress, there were two assessments: an oral pre-assessment at the beginning of a five-week program and a post-assessment at 
the conclusion. A teacher's guide, student materials, and automated scoring sheets are included in the assessment resources.  
The resources for delivering the Grade 1 Rapid Mathematics Assessment were included in the booklet. It was ready to e nsure that the 
required resources were accessible when the test was administered. Most of the tasks in this evaluation call for respondents to work 
with cutouts, pictures, and objects. The participants responded to the assessment tasks orally. On the mater ials, they did not write. For 
most assessment tasks, one set of materials might be adequate for every participant, although some might require copying and cutting. 
It's recommended to paste these materials on cardboard and use them again. The researcher was responsible for making sure all required 
materials were ready and for reading the Teacher's Booklet that goes with the test. It was anticipated that the researcher was acquainted 
with the resources, including how to use them and what to use them for.

relationship with the respondents. The researcher started the conduct of Rapid Mathematics Assessment, both pretest and postt est, by

Procedure

and School Principal. It took place in the second quarter of the School Year 2024 -2025 at Andales Integrated School, Initao North 
District, Division of Misamis Oriental.  
With the learners' academic performance during the first quarter, the participants were chosen based on their grades. Those l earners 
who garnered 75-79 (fairly satisfactory) and 80-84 (satisfactory) grades were the participants of the study. They had the pretest using

Data Analysis 
To obtain the actual details required, the data were tallied and evaluated. The subsequent statistical instruments were utilized to address 
the various issues raised:  
For Problems 1 and 2, Frequency and Percentage were used to determine the pretest and posttest scores of the participants.  
For Problem 3, a Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test was used to test the significant difference between the pretest and posttest scores of the 
participants.

Problem 4. What action plan can be designed based on the results of the study?  
I. Rationale  
Numeracy in early childhood education served as the foundations for later school achievement, including, but not limited to, problem-
solving abilities. Example, counting, addition, and subtraction were some of the most important numeracy skills for a child's first-grade 
cognitive and ac ademic progress (LC). Unfortunately, there were a lot of us smart young learners who, because of inadequately 
pedagogical content, do not wished to learn such fundamental stuff due to lack of interest and engagement and because they we re not 
compelling and experiential. 
As a result of the increasing digital world, there was a tendency to apply Digital Game-Based Learning (DGBL) in a practical form, by 
using it to teach numeracy in a classroom environment. Several studies suggested that interactive computer games have the ability to 
produce, facilitate, and deliver relevant feedback to young learners. Hence, the learners learned the knowledge at the speed that they 
judged to be appropriate for them, as dictated by the individual differences in the learning speed the students exhibit. Another effective 
way that gaming can help overcome "mathemaphobia" was by either promoting a sense of mastery over the topic or by making 
mathematics appealing and preventing a bad experience.

Conclusions 
This study assessed the contribution of digital game-based learning on Grade 1 learners' numeracy skills in the school year 2024-2025 
at Andales Integrated School, Initao North District, Division of Misamis Oriental. The results indicated that DGBL could be employed 
as a useful method to enhance the development of learners' numeracy skills, considering the vast improvement in the post -test score 
upon its implementation. The majority of learners showed noticeable progress from their pretest to posttest results, with a shift towards 
higher proficiency categories. This suggested that digital games, when integrated effectively into the learning process, can enhance 
learner engagement, understanding, and application of mathematical concepts.  
Overall, the large variation between pretest and posttest scores, as established by the Wilcoxon Signed -Rank Test, represented how 
actual Digital Game-Based Learning really was in its approach to teaching numeracy development. However, while the learners have 
made measurable advancements, some were still not at the "At Grade Level" standard. This meant that more support and strategi es 
may need to be employed to ensure that all le arners achieve full proficiency. In so doing, the importance of using technology in 
education lies more so in early grade levels, where it fosters a fun and active learning environment that encourages active participation 
and building foundational skills. 
This study highlighted the potential of digital game -based learning in revolutionizing traditional pedagogy, thereby affording more 
immersive and personalized learning. It also opens up worthwhile insights to educators, school administrators, and policymak ers on 
the actual teaching-learning advantages that could be reaped by simply tapping into educational technology. The findings of this study 
suggested that involving digital games in the classroom improved the numeracy skills of first -grade learners as well as their academic 
performance. Now, since schools are embracing advancements in technology, it became even more important to discuss other ways  
digital game-based learning can be developed and used to accomplish the task of developing different abilitie s in learners.
