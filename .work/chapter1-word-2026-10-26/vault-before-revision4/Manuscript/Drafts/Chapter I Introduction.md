This chapter presents the context, purpose, objectives, scope, and limitations of Land Grand. The proposed educational game focuses on landforms, rocks, and minerals for Grade 5 learners at Baluan Elementary School.

## Project Context

The proposed project concerns supplementary Science learning at home and continuity of learning when classroom instruction is disrupted. Land Grand is intended to give Grade 5 learners another way to encounter landforms, rocks, and minerals alongside printed learning materials. Its rationale considers international guidance on educational technology, Philippine distance-learning experience, and reported needs within the school community.

International discussions of educational technology emphasize the relationship between a tool and the learning need it is intended to address. UNESCO's 2023 Global Education Monitoring Report recommends assessing technology for its suitability to local conditions, inclusion, supporting evidence, and sustainability. It also places learners and teachers at the centre of decisions about educational technology. These considerations support examining whether a proposed resource fits its intended users before assuming that digital delivery improves learning (UNESCO, 2023).

For Land Grand, this guidance directs attention to the proposed learning activities, access requirements, and role of adult support. An exploration game would need to present relevant content and connect its activities with the concepts learners are expected to encounter. Its educational value would require evaluation beyond the presence of animations, rewards, or completed levels. The project therefore treats interaction as a proposed means of supporting learning rather than evidence that learning has already improved.

In the Philippines, distance-learning arrangements during the COVID-19 period included printed and digital modular approaches. The Department of Education's 2020 guidance described these approaches and allowed supplementary resources, including interactive electronic materials. It also emphasized selecting arrangements appropriate to learners' circumstances and providing support from household learning facilitators. These historical guidelines provide a national context for supplementary home learning without establishing the present delivery arrangement of any particular school (Department of Education, 2020, Appendix A, items 1, 3, 6, and 12; Appendix B, section A).

Disaster-related disruption is also relevant to the project's regional context. DOST-PHIVOLCS documented a magnitude 7.8 earthquake offshore Sarangani on June 8, 2026 (DOST-PHIVOLCS, 2026). The project clarification dated October 26, 2026, states that Baluan Elementary School currently holds face-to-face classes. It identifies modular learning as a contingency if another strong earthquake or other event disrupts classroom instruction. Land Grand is consequently proposed as supplementary home-learning support during regular schooling and potential disruptions, rather than a response to a claimed ongoing closure.

Local survey information adds context to this proposed use. The school survey was conducted on July 7, 2026, according to the corrected project clarification. The July 13 Google Form version was used to organize and export the same survey data. These dates describe field administration and subsequent digital preparation, respectively ([[Sources/Project Clarifications 2026-10-26|project clarification]], 2026).

The project clarification describes respondent selection as randomized. Parent participation covered Grades 4–6 because those grades had a scheduled parents' orientation meeting. This identifies the available parent group rather than a sampling frame encompassing every grade in the school.

The parent CSV contains six records for children in Grade 4, six in Grade 5, and eight in Grade 6. The teacher CSV contains two Kindergarten records and four, five, five, and four records for Grades 3, 4, 5, and 6, respectively. Each group therefore contains 20 records, with no Grade 1 or Grade 2 entries. The figures reported below summarize each full group rather than isolate the Grade 5 records ([[Sources/Baluan Survey CSV Records|survey response exports]]; [[Sources/Baluan Survey Tally|Tally - Baluan]], n.d.).

The parent responses indicate a difficulty relevant to support at home. Seventeen of 20 responses, or 85%, answered Yes to difficulty explaining module lessons to their child. Two answered No, and one answered Maybe. This finding supports considering additional explanatory material for home use without implying that parents lack interest in their children's education (Tally - Baluan, n.d., parents Part I, question 3).

Science difficulty ratings also relate to the project's subject area. Seventeen of 20 parent ratings and 13 of 20 teacher ratings placed Science at 4 or 5. The source defines these categories as Difficult and Very Difficult. Its topic-frequency table includes Rocks / Kinds of Rocks / Rock Formation with frequency 3, Minerals with 1, and Landforms with 1. These entries establish reported mentions, while their respondent grouping and coding require confirmation. They do not show that these topics are the greatest difficulties among Grade 5 learners (Tally - Baluan, n.d., parent and teacher subject-rating tables; Science topic-frequency table).

Responses about mobile learning provide a basis for considering the proposed delivery format. Fourteen of 20 parent responses indicated that a mobile game could support printed modules, compared with two No and four Maybe responses. Fifteen of 20 teacher responses indicated that a mobile game could enhance understanding alongside modules, compared with one No and four Maybe responses. These responses express expectations about a possible resource rather than results from using Land Grand (Tally - Baluan, n.d., parents Part I, question 8; teachers Part I, question 6).

Access and willingness also need consideration. Eighteen parent responses indicated household access to a smartphone, tablet, or computer; one answered No, and one answered Maybe. Thirteen indicated willingness to allow a mobile learning game, while five answered No and two answered Maybe. The combined device-access question does not establish Android compatibility or availability for every intended learner. These responses support considering a supplementary resource while retaining other learning arrangements (Tally - Baluan, n.d., parents Part I, questions 4–5).

The following reported concerns and proposed design needs define the project's focus. The first two come from the tally, while the latter two are design responses informed by the survey and continuity context.

- **P-01:** Parents reported difficulty explaining lessons in printed modules, supporting consideration of additional explanations for home learning.
- **P-02:** Parent and teacher ratings identified perceived Science difficulty across their represented grades, supporting a focused resource without implying a Grade 5 test deficit.
- **P-03:** Responses about supplementing printed modules provide a rationale for an interactive learning activity, whose usefulness still requires evaluation.
- **P-04:** The contingency use of modular learning creates a design need for supplementary activities that can operate offline and retain progress between sessions.

The reported difficulty assisting with modules supports developing an additional resource during regular schooling, before another disruption requires greater reliance on home study. The proposed game connects short explanations with exploration, collection, and review activities focused on the selected Science content. It is intended to supplement teacher guidance and printed materials during home study. Development would provide a resource for subsequent evaluation, while any claim of improved knowledge or engagement would require evidence from an appropriate study.

## Purpose and Description of the Project

Land Grand is proposed as an offline Android educational game for Grade 5 learners at Baluan Elementary School. Its purpose is to support knowledge of landforms, rocks, and minerals through short lessons and interactive exploration. The game is intended for supplementary home use alongside the school's printed learning materials.

The proposed design contains four episodes covering Mountains, Hills, Plains, and Plateaus. Before each level, the learner would read a short lesson about the landform and selected rocks and minerals. During exploration, the learner would move an explorer character, collect items, and read their names and descriptions. A result screen would present collected items, a short review, and gameplay stars after level completion. The application would retain progress on the device for later sessions.

The design brings these activities together for a defined audience and content area. Its relevance lies in the proposed connection between Science explanations, exploration, and review within an offline learning activity. The survey responses inform this rationale, while the game's effect on learners remains to be evaluated. Local progress records would show game activity rather than independently verified academic achievement.

## Objectives of the Project

The objectives specify the application deliverables intended to address the reported concerns and design needs. They describe proposed functions rather than outcomes already demonstrated through testing.

The general objective is to develop Land Grand for Grade 5 learners at Baluan Elementary School. The offline Android exploration game is intended to support knowledge of landforms, rocks, and minerals. Development is targeted for completion by **[confirmed project completion date]**.

The specific objectives are the following:

- **O-01:** Provide a short lesson before each level and display the name and description of each collected rock or mineral.
- **O-02:** Develop four landform episodes covering Mountains, Hills, Plains, and Plateaus, with selected rocks and minerals relevant to the intended Grade 5 content.

## Scope and Limitations of the Project

This section defines the proposed application's coverage, deliverables, and boundaries. The development scope concerns supplementary learning about the selected Science topics for Grade 5 learners at Baluan Elementary School.

### Scope

The project proposes a single-player Android game developed using Unity and C#, with Piskel and Clip Studio Paint for graphical assets. Its proposed scope includes the following:

- Four episodes representing Mountains, Hills, Plains, and Plateaus, together with selected rocks and minerals associated with the covered content.
- Easy, Normal, and Hard difficulty modes containing 3, 5, and 7 levels per episode, respectively. This proposed inventory totals 60 levels across four episodes.
- Short lessons before each level, item names and descriptions during collection, and review material after completion.
- Touch controls for moving and jumping, with exploration and collection activities in a 2D game environment.
- Challenges such as obstacles, traps, pitfalls, and time limits, with replay and continuation to available levels.
- Progression through difficulty modes and episodes, with a landform summary and a final completion screen.
- Up to three gameplay stars after each level, with the award criteria and completion rules requiring confirmation.
- A Main Menu with Play, Settings, Tutorial, and Exit, together with a Landform Map for selecting unlocked episodes.
- Local saving and loading of the player's name, unlocked episodes, completed levels, stars, and collected items.
- Offline operation on Android phones and tablets running Android 10 and above. The pre-proposal lists minimum targets of 2 GB RAM and 300 MB free storage; compatibility remains subject to device testing.

The confirmed development and study period must be entered before final submission. Detailed scoring, unlocking, retry, and save-recovery rules require specification before they can be treated as final requirements.

### Limitations

The application and study have the following proposed boundaries:

- The intended audience is Grade 5 learners at Baluan Elementary School. Survey figures in this chapter aggregate each respondent group across its represented grades.
- Content is limited to the four selected landforms and selected related rocks and minerals, rather than all Earth Science topics.
- The game excludes multiplayer, online features, online accounts, cloud storage, websites, and an administrator panel.
- Progress remains on the device being used. The proposal does not provide centralized teacher monitoring or a shared learner-record system.
- Learning activities exclude separate quiz screens, GPS features, and real-world scanning.
- The application supplements printed materials and teacher guidance. Gameplay stars and completion records do not independently measure learning gains or replace an academic assessment.
- The survey's perceptions and expectations do not establish the effectiveness of Land Grand. Conclusions about learning outcomes require a defined evaluation method and actual results.
